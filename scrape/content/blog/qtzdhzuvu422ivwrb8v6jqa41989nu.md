# Little Known HOA Requirements for Exterior Painting

Apr 3

Written By Chris Petkau

Thinking about a fresh new look for your home? You found the perfect color scheme on Pinterest and you’re ready to hire professional exterior painters in Fort Bend County—but there’s one thing standing in your way: the Homeowners Association (HOA).
Before you head to the paint store, make sure your color choices are approved. Here’s everything you need to know about HOA painting requirements and how to avoid costly mistakes.

Why HOA Compliance Matters

Skipping HOA approval for your exterior painting project can result in fines—and worse, being forced to repaint your home. That means paying twice for a job that could have been done right the first time.
Fortunately, this headache can be easily avoided. Most HOAs require a simple approval process, which usually includes:

- Filling out a request form

- Submitting a color swatch or sample

- Waiting for official approval before work begins

Although it may take a few days to hear back, it’s well worth avoiding unnecessary drama and delays.

### ✅ Step 1: Contact Your HOA Early
The most proactive step you can take is to reach out to your HOA before scheduling your residential exterior painting. Until you receive approval, your project can’t legally move forward.

### ✅ Step 2: Understand the Restrictions
Most HOAs avoid loud or bright paint colors. They often want a cohesive look throughout the neighborhood. Some have a pre-approved palette, while others just want to review your selection to ensure it’s consistent with surrounding homes.

### ✅ Step 3: Look Around Your Neighborhood
Take inspiration from homes nearby.

- Did your neighbor recently limewash red brick into a soft white?

- Is your street filled with earthy, neutral tones or clean, modern grays?

- Has you neighbors fence recently been stained?

Look for color schemes that fit your style and neighborhood. Submitting similar shades can speed up the approval process.

## Submitting Your Colors to the HOA

Once you’ve done your research:

- Choose a color scheme that aligns with HOA guidelines

- Include swatches or manufacturer codes

- Submit your form well before your painters are scheduled to begin

Often, you'll get approved on the first try—especially if the colors are in line with what’s already in your neighborhood.
