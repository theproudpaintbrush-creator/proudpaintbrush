Make it New

First, make sure to pressure wash the fence with a mild detergent, removing all dirt and debris. Then sand all rough spots. Once that’s done, you’re ready to start the staining process. Make sure to use the highest quality of stain to ensure lasting durability. Also take into account the climate conditions, as you’ll want to select an appropriate stain that can handle harsh weather. 

Make it Last

To get the most out of the stain, it is important to prep the fence. Clean the fence with a pressure washer if possible, or use a stiff brush, detergent, and a bucket of water to scrub away any dirt, dust, and debris. Allow the fence to dry thoroughly before you begin staining. If the fence has mold or mildew, you may need to treat it with a specially-formulated cleaner. After cleaning and drying, sand the wood to create a smooth surface. Finally, apply a primer before you begin applying the stain. 

Make it Stand Out

Oil-based stains are longer-lasting, more durable and offer more protection for the wood, making them the preferred choice for outdoor wood projects. Oil-based stains are more difficult to apply and may require multiple coats for full coverage. Water-based stains offer easier cleanup and less odor, but the color and protection may not last as long as oil-based stains. Both types of stain must be applied to a clean, dry surface and may require priming to ensure coverage.

Make it Beautiful

When applying the stain, use a brush to ensure an even coat. This will help you get into all the nooks and crannies of your fence. The best way to apply the stain is in long strokes, starting at the bottom and working your way up and over. Begin each stroke at the opposite end of the previous stroke to make sure you get consistent coverage. Once the stain is dry, you can apply a sealer to protect your work and make it last longer against the elements.
