Final pricing depends on prep requirements, cabinet condition, finish expectations, and the level of detail involved.

## Why Cabinet Painting Costs More Than Walls
Cabinet painting is not the same as painting walls.
It requires significantly more prep, precision, and controlled application.

### What makes cabinets more labor-intensive:

- Removing and reinstalling doors and hardware

- Cleaning and degreasing surfaces

- Detailed sanding and surface prep

- Priming for adhesion and durability

- Controlled spraying for a smooth finish

- Multiple coats with proper drying time

This is why cabinet painting costs more — and why shortcuts lead to peeling, chipping, and early failure.

## Our Cabinet Painting Process
This is where the difference is made.

### Step 1: Remove & Label
We carefully remove doors, drawers, and hardware, labeling everything for precise reinstallation.

### Step 2: Deep Cleaning & Prep
Cabinets are degreased, sanded, and prepped to ensure proper adhesion.

### Step 3: Surface Repair
We address minor imperfections and prepare surfaces for a smooth finish.

### Step 4: Prime
A bonding primer is applied to create a strong foundation.

### Step 5: Spray Finish Application
We use a controlled spray system to achieve a smooth, factory-like finish.

### Step 6: Curing & Reinstallation
Doors are cured properly before being reinstalled and adjusted.

## What Affects Cabinet Painting Cost
Several factors determine where your project falls within the price range:

- Number of doors and drawers

- Cabinet condition (damage, wear, previous coatings)

- Wood type and surface texture

- Color change (especially dark to light)

- Finish level expectations

- Layout complexity (islands, built-ins, detailed trim)

- Hardware removal and reinstallation
